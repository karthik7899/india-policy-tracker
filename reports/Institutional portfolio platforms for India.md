# Unlicensed data, not analytics, blocks institutional use

The institutional platforms sell managers of Indian listed equities three things the India Policy Tracker does not have: data they are licensed to use and show to others, an India factor risk model with Brinson and factor attribution, and controlled workflows (order management with pre-trade compliance, surveillance and audit trails) that SEBI's rules for asset management companies all but require. The tracker's new portfolio layer already computes many of the numbers SEBI and AMFI make funds disclose, from beta and volatility to the days needed to sell 25% and 50% of a book and limit breaches, but not by the regulators' methods and from inputs it has no right to use: NSE's website terms expressly prohibit "systematic or automated data collection", which is how the tracker reads announcements, delivery data, the event calendar and corporate actions every day, and AMFI's terms confine its NAVs to personal, non-commercial use. That provenance problem, together with a public repository that has no accounts, roles or audit trail, blocks every type of institution before any missing analytic matters; beyond it, mutual fund AMCs most need SEBI/AMFI production jobs and limits summed across all schemes, PMS providers need time-weighted returns per investment approach against an APMI benchmark, FPIs need MSCI benchmarks, a global risk model and dollar returns, and insurers need assets beyond Indian equities (IRDAI's rules were not researched). None of the surveyed products documents what the tracker does best (reading central and nine state governments' policy for its effect on named holdings, tracing second-order effects through an entity graph, and grading a written thesis per holding), which makes it a credible research add-on beside a large institution's systems and a plausible main platform for a boutique. The practical path is licensed NSE data first, at about ₹6–12 lakh a year†, then a private deployment with real books and the regulatory calculators, and only then a risk model, which runs from about ₹24–30 lakh a year for one Bloomberg seat† to an estimated ₹43 lakh–₹2.4 crore for BarraOne†. No primary source page could be opened during the research, so every figure here is a search-engine extract, and those marked † must be checked against the primary document before anyone relies on them.

### The Indian data underneath is mostly public

Bloomberg has drawn shareholding patterns, results and governance data from BSE's XBRL filings since March 2019 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/?p=3376)), LSEG takes NSE prices directly from the exchange with history from 1994 ([LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-catalogue/equities/pricing/equities-national-stock-exchange-of-india-nsei-ll)), and FactSet chooses which companies to cover by "Market Capitalization, Index Constituents, Broker Coverage, Size, and Importance of the Market" ([Xignite listing of FactSet Fundamentals](https://cmsstaging.xignite.com/wiki/49)), a rule that thins coverage exactly where an Indian small-cap manager works. What the global vendors add is normalisation, long history and, above all, consensus drawn from contributor networks. I/B/E/S claims **22,000+ active companies and 950+ contributing firms "from large global houses to regional and local brokers"** ([LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-catalogue/company-data/ibes-estimates/broker-estimates)), S&P had **60 Indian research providers** in its research collection by March 2024 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/hdfc-securities-investment-research-now-available-through-sp-capital-iq-pro)), and Bloomberg consensus is what Nomura used in August 2026 to track earnings revisions for the BSE 200+ universe ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/nifty-at-25900-by-march-2027-nomura-keeps-index-target-shares-key-themes-to-watch-549936-2026-08-19)).

### Only two risk models are built for India, and neither is published

MSCI's **INE2** (the Barra India Total Market Equity Model) is described only on a third-party help page, which says it updates style, industry and market factors **daily**, with styles including momentum, liquidity, profitability, leverage, value, growth and volatility ([Arcana help centre](https://help.arcana.io/en/articles/14983275-msci-barra-india-total-market-equity-model-ine2-methodology-handbook-pdf)); its estimation universe, factor count and release date were not found, and the handbook appears to be available to clients only. Bloomberg's MAC3 suite lists **India among 13 local equity models** beside a global model ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/insights/data/the-benefits-and-impact-of-including-satellite-factors-in-mac3-equity-models/)), and MAC3 as a whole now has **3,000+ factors, six forecast horizons and 800+ clients** ([PR Newswire, April 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bloomberg-expands-mac3-risk-models-for-enhanced-portfolio-and-risk-forecasting-across-public-and-private-investments-302732596.html)), but the India model's factors and universe could not be retrieved either.

Every other model treats India as a single country. MSCI's GEMLT spans **87 countries with one set of 16 style factors** ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/242721/GEMLT_FactSheet.pdf)), Axioma's previous worldwide model gave a local market factor only to Domestic China ([Axioma factsheet](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2174119/Return%20Downloads/Factsheet-AXWW4-2.pdf)), Northfield's list of single-country models has no India entry ([Northfield](https://www.northinfo.com/searchreg.php?keywords=asset)), and FactSet's own global daily model has 52 country factors ([FactSet](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Brochures/factset-equity-model-global-daily-brochure.pdf)). That structure suits an FPI, whose India risk must add up with the rest of an emerging-markets book, and suits an India-only mandate badly, because that mandate's active risk sits in domestic size, value and momentum effects that a global model does not estimate separately.

Small caps are the weak spot. MSCI India IMI, the obvious anchor for an estimation universe, has only **626 constituents** ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/indexes/index/664231/msci-india-imi-index)), and an academic case study on SBI Mutual Fund reportedly found BarraOne's and Bloomberg's ex-ante tracking errors diverging for the same portfolio, a conclusion the notes could not confirm ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3635138)). No Indian commercial factor-model vendor was found. The free IIM Ahmedabad Fama-French and momentum library, built from CMIE Prowess data with history from October 1993 and three releases a year, gives factor returns but no covariance forecast and no stock-specific risk ([IIMA](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/)).

### Attribution and stress testing are commodities; SEBI's liquidity measure is not

Every major vendor offers attribution and stress testing. FactSet advertises **more than 10 attribution models**, Brinson and factor-based ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/hubfs/Resources%20Section/Brochures/portfolio-analysis-brochure.pdf)); MSCI's BarraOne offers Brinson-Fachler allocation and selection alongside factor attribution ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/documents/1296102/8335426/MSCI+MAC+Performance+Attribution+Factsheet.pdf)); and Bloomberg computes VaR by historical, parametric and Monte Carlo methods and runs factor, full-valuation, macroeconomic and climate scenarios ([Bloomberg](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/bloomberg-terminal/portfolio-analytics/)). The 2024–26 frontier is language models laid over these numbers: FactSet's Portfolio Commentary (April 2024) drafts attribution narratives with "auditable connections" between each sentence and the figures behind it ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/04/30/2872173/7768/en/factset-introduces-ai-powered-portfolio-commentary.html)), Bloomberg added AI commentary to PORT Enterprise in 2025 and cited **750+ PORT Enterprise clients** at launch ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/bloomberg-advances-portfolio-analytics-with-launch-of-ai-portfolio-commentary-in-port-enterprise)), and Axioma now lets users propose stress scenarios in plain language ([SimCorp](https://www.simcorp.com/about-us/news/2026/ai-powered-stress-testing)).

Liquidity is the exception that matters for India. Bloomberg's LQA models liquidation cost and time from redemption size, available volume, volatility and spread, but its brochures are built around European (ESMA) and Japanese rules ([Bloomberg](https://data.bloomberglp.com/professional/sites/10/ESMA-Liquidity-Stress-Testing-Brochure.pdf)), and MSCI positions LiquidityMetrics for UCITS, AIFMD, SEC Rule 22e-4 and Form PF ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/documents/1296102/8335426/MSCI_LiquidityMetrics_Factsheet.pdf/1ef81401-d1d4-6e84-834c-f03d3225c318)). Neither computes the SEBI/AMFI measure, so even well-equipped AMCs produce the Indian days-to-liquidate figure separately, and a small India-specific tool can own that calculation.

### Order management is the hardest layer to replace, and the only one with named Indian users

Bloomberg AIM embeds pre- and post-trade checks in the order workflow through its Compliance Manager, a rules engine with a violation blotter and a "split and send" workflow for orders that would breach a rule, which won a 2025 industry award and which one client credits with **750+ pre-coded rule templates** ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/awards-rankings/7952788/bst-awards-2025-best-buy-side-compliance-product-trading-bloomberg)). **Five Indian AMCs are publicly named as AIM users**: Aditya Birla Sun Life from 2020, extended to its GIFT City unit in 2022 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/aditya-birla-sun-life-amc-becomes-indias-first-asset-manager-to-expand-adoption-of-bloomberg-aim-to-its-gift-city-unit)); Mirae Asset from 2022, with the Vault communications archive ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/mirae-asset-investment-managers-india-adopts-bloomberg-solutions-for-the-buy-side/)); DSP from 2022, with PORT Enterprise, on a press page the notes could not isolate† ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/?p=28496)); NJ from 2023, also with Vault ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/nj-asset-management-streamlines-order-management-and-compliance-management-with-bloomberg-solutions/)); and Bajaj Finserv from 2023 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/bajaj-finserv-asset-management-adopts-bloomberg-aim-to-power-digitization-journey)). Three of the five (Mirae, NJ and Bajaj Finserv) are newer or foreign-parented houses, which fits a pattern of buying an integrated stack rather than assembling one.

FactSet has assembled a rival front office from the Portware EMS and LiquidityBook, which it bought in February 2025 for **$246.5M**, gaining FIX links to more than 200 brokers ([FactSet](https://investor.factset.com/news-releases/news-release-details/factset-acquires-liquiditybook)); nothing found shows connectivity to Indian brokers, and no Indian Charles River client turned up. Beyond that, evidence of Indian adoption is thin. Morningstar Direct reported "around 30 subscribers in India" in an undated interview ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/9518-use-star-ratings-as-a-first-level-check-but-dont-blindly-follow-them-aditya-agarwal-morningstar)), DICGC, the RBI's deposit insurer, buys Refinitiv, Bloomberg and Cogencis without tender because "there are no reasonable substitute/alternatives" ([DICGC](https://www.dicgc.org.in/sites/default/files/2026-04/work-issued-on-nomination-basis.pdf)), and Morgan Stanley's Mumbai attribution team asks for FactSet, Aladdin and Bloomberg experience ([job listing](https://militaryspouse.dejobs.org/mumbai-ind/analyst-associate-gpar-investment-management/C155BB70FBE94E9CB5B3B7CD5CD747F6/job)).

### ESG and benchmarks are gated by Indian regulation, not vendor capability

SEBI's rules for ESG schemes require monthly statements to show security-wise BRSR Core scores from **SEBI-registered ESG rating providers** ([SEBI circular copy](https://www.skylinerta.com/pdf_file/51_671021621_SEBICircular-ESG-DisclosurebyMutualFund-20.07.2023.pdf)). MSCI's Indian entity registered as a Category II, subscriber-pays provider on **13 August 2024**† ([SEBI board memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/dec-2024/1735215341884_1.pdf)), while secondary sources say LSEG and Bloomberg withdrew the ESG scores they had published on Indian companies because they were not registered† ([Rajagiri Business School](https://www.rajagiribusinessschool.edu.in/blog-details/esg-ratings-in-india)). FPIs are measured against the MSCI India family, whose weights depend on foreign-ownership limits and "foreign room", the gap between foreign holdings and the limit ([MSCI methodology](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Feb15_GIMIMethod_with_India_Dom_Final.pdf)). India's weight in MSCI Emerging Markets fell from roughly 19–21% in September 2024 to about 11–12% by April–May 2026† ([Moneycontrol via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:63f5cf591094b:0-india-s-weight-in-msci-em-index-falls-to-near-covid-lows-as-flows-shift/)), turning each index review into a passive-flow event. Domestic funds benchmark to NSE, BSE and CRISIL indices instead, and SEBI's draft list of "significant indices" named those three providers and not MSCI ([Regstreet Law](https://regstreetlaw.com/blog/sebi-issues-consultation-paper-on-significant-indices-under-index-provider-regulations-2024/)).

## Indian vendors sell licensed, standardised data for a fraction of the price

### Fundamentals databases sell the licence the tracker lacks

India's database vendors sell the same public filings in standardised form under a licence, which is exactly what the tracker lacks. CMIE's Prowess carries time series from 1990 for every NSE- and BSE-listed company plus unlisted ones, with bulk delivery through Prowess dx ([IIM Indore guide](https://iimidr.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/CMIE-Prowess-dx.pdf)). Accord Fintech's ACE Equity Nxt covers about **40,000 companies and 1,750 data fields**, including shareholding and corporate actions ([IIM Trichy library](https://library.iimtrichy.ac.in/ace-equity/)), and Accord calls itself an authorised data-feed vendor of the exchanges, delivering over FTP and API according to a third-party page ([Accord Fintech](https://www.accordfintech.com/market-data-feed); [FintegrationFS](https://www.fintegrationfs.com/fintechapis/accord-fintech)). Capitaline claims **35,000+ companies** ([ICAI](https://www.icai.org/post/16361)) and is listed by a company database as a brand of the API vendor CMOTS, which is unverified ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/legal-entities/india/cmots-internet-technologies-private-limited/__z4Azg2QgkuXosScB3dH4EdCnxslDBWTQUM321QEKW0s)). CRISIL's Quantix covers **60,000+ companies** classified by GICS and now sits inside i360, a "GenAI-led" platform launched in February 2026 with an "impact-analytics engine" called ForeSight ([Crisil](https://crisil.com/en/home/our-businesses/quantix.html); [LatestLY/PR Newswire](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/business-news-crisil-launches-i360-a-unified-genai-led-research-data-and-analytics-platform-7330957.html/amp)).

Prices are by quote. The only public anchors are association discounts such as **₹25,000 per login a year** for Capitaline's cloud product under a deal with ICAI, the accountants' institute† ([ICAI](https://www.icai.org/post/16361)), and rates like that understate institutional and redistribution licences. No source says whether any of these vendors offers point-in-time, as-first-reported fundamentals, the property a backtest or a fair track record most needs, so that has to be asked for in writing.

### Consensus exists only for the top 900–1,000 companies

Consensus thins out quickly below the large caps, whoever supplies it. Trendlyne's Forecaster, the only documented domestic aggregator, covers **about 900 companies** according to its help page, though a separate FAQ says about 3,000†; its pages show 36 analysts on Nestlé India and 8 on Data Patterns, and it publishes no list of contributing brokers ([Trendlyne](https://help.trendlyne.com/support/solutions/articles/84000383175-what-are-forecaster-or-analyst-estimates-)). Value Research put the uncovered tail at "**over 5300 of the 6000-odd stocks**" listed in India, in an undated piece† ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/18224/a-basic-disconnect/)). Usable consensus therefore exists for roughly the top 900–1,000 companies, and the tracker's refusal to call a result a beat or a miss without consensus is the same constraint a Bloomberg user meets further down the market-cap scale.

### Fund data and back-office tools are local businesses

AMFI's NAVs are free but licensed for "personal non-commercial use" only, with public display, transmission, derivative works and storing "any significant portion" of the site all barred ([AMFI terms](https://www.amfiindia.com/terms-of-use)). Commercial users buy standardised fund data instead: from CRISIL, whose fund ranking has run since June 2000 and whose Fund Analyser covers 6,500+ schemes ([Crisil](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/what-we-do/research/investment-research-product/mutual-fund-research.html)); from ICRA Analytics, whose MFI 360 costs ₹25,000 a year at the CFA Society India member rate† ([CFA Society India](https://cfasocietyindia.org/wp-content/uploads/Media-Uploads-Membership/Member-offers/Member-Offer-Document_ICRA-A_MFI360.pdf)); or from Accord's ACE MF, Capitaline's NAV India, Value Research and Morningstar. On the operations side, KFintech bought Hexagram in February 2022 for its mPower fund-accounting and iMatch reconciliation products, whose clients include mutual funds, AIFs and insurers ([KFintech](https://investor.kfintech.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02-09-2022-KFintech-acquires-Hexagram-to-expand-into-Fund-Accounting-Press-Release.pdf)); Valuefy sells portfolio and risk management with performance attribution to banks, AMCs and family offices ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/companies/valuefy/amp)); and Finalyca shows PMS, AIF, mutual fund and ULIP analytics on one screen ([Finalyca](https://finalyca.com)). The notes could not verify Indian institutional clients for any other order-management, compliance or fund-accounting vendor, global or Indian: SS&C, FIS, SimCorp, Wealth Spectrum, Fundtec, Investwell, CAMS, TCS BaNCS and 63 moons all remain unverified leads.

The local AI-research niche, the competitive frame for the tracker's language-model work, is filling fast. Zerodha invested about $5M in Tijori in November 2025 as Tijori pointed roughly 70% of its roadmap at institutions, led by Call Monitor, an AI tool that summarises earnings calls ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/zerodha-invests-5-mn-in-tijori-10817854)), and InferEdge is building "agentic AI for the buy-side" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/ai/india)). Retail tools are cheap individual seats: Screener costs ₹4,999 a year and Trendlyne ₹1,890–11,900, and no source found grants redistribution rights for either ([Screener](https://screener.in/gold); [FindMyMoat](https://www.findmymoat.com/vs/stock-events-vs-trendlyne)).

## SEBI and AMFI turn analytics into dated production jobs

### The 2026 rewrite favours configurable limits

SEBI rewrote the mutual fund rulebook in 2026, and the change favours configurable platforms over hard-coded ones. The SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 replaced the 1996 Regulations on **1 April 2026**, a new Master Circular dated **20 March 2026** replaced the 2024 one, and investment restrictions moved to a Sixth Schedule that leaves the single-company concentration limit to SEBI's "prudential norms" instead of a fixed percentage ([ELP](https://elplaw.in/leadership/sebi-revamps-and-replaces-its-30-year-old-regulations-for-mutual-funds/); [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/53893/sebi-issued-the-master-circular-for-mutual-funds/)). The notes could not confirm whether the familiar numbers survive (10% of a scheme's NAV in one company, 10% of a company's voting capital across all of a fund's schemes, and 25% of net assets in the sponsor's group)† ([TaxManagementIndia](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_act.asp?ID=35302); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/equity-oriented-etfs-and-index-funds-can-invest-in-listed-securities-of-sponsors-group-companies-beyond-25-sebi/)), so a platform has to hold limits as parameters and sum them across every scheme in the fund house. The rest of the regime turns analytics into jobs with deadlines and named owners; the table sets each against the tracker as it stood on 10 October 2026.

### The AMFI stress test is the most consequential calculation

It is also the most India-specific. Since March 2024, at SEBI's request, small- and mid-cap schemes have published the days needed to sell 25% and 50% of the portfolio pro rata, now monthly by the 15th ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/mutual-fund/mfs-told-to-disclose-stress-test-reports-of-midcap-smallcap-schemes-124022901229_1.html); [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/a-better-way-to-stress-test-small-and-mid-cap-mfs/)). On the secondary summaries available, the method drops the least-liquid 20% of the portfolio and assumes 10% participation in three-month average volume on NSE and BSE combined, with that volume multiplied by three† ([PersonalFN](https://www.personalfn.com/dwl/Mutual-Funds/what-is-the-capacity-and-liquidity-of-your-small-cap-fund); [Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/amp/story/business%2Fmarkets%2Fwhy-the-ongoing-mf-stress-tests-should-not-worry-you-2951143)). The answers carry real consequences: SBI Small Cap needed **59 days** to sell half its book in May 2026 and HDFC Mid Cap 34 ([Bajaj Broking](https://www.bajajbroking.in/share-market-news/midcap-and-smallcap-fund-stress-test-may-2026-data)), while Quant Small Cap needed **48 days** on July 2026 data ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/mutual-funds/small-cap-fund-stress-test-schemes-with-greater-midcap-exposure-fare-better-on-liquidity/article-199276/)). Some AMCs add their own triggers on top; Taurus reviews a scheme when days-to-liquidate rise more than 50% on the previous month or quarter ([Taurus MF](https://taurusmutualfund.com:443/sites/default/files/2024-03/Final_Policy_on_Mid-Cap_and_Small-Cap_Schemes.pdf)).

### Governance rules require records a research tool does not keep

The governance rules matter more than their metrics, because they require records. The June 2025 passive-breach circular gives a scheme 30 business days to fix any passive breach, then requires a written justification to the Investment Committee, allows an extension of up to 60 business days and bars new scheme launches while the breach stands† ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/06/30/sebi-mutual-fund-passive-breach-rebalancing-timeline-update/)). Since 2020 dealers may speak only on recorded lines, recordings must be reviewed and kept for at least **eight years**, and a board-approved trade allocation policy is required ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-asks-mfs-to-put-in-place-policy-on-trade-execution-allocation-120091701311_1.html)). Since August 2024 AMCs must also run alert-based surveillance against front-running and insider trading that takes account of recorded chats, access logs and CCTV, with the CEO and Chief Compliance Officer accountable ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/32011-sebi-directs-amcs-to-put-in-place-a-mechanism-to-curb-front-running)). These duties are the likeliest reason Mirae and NJ bought the Vault archive alongside AIM, though no source says so.

### Other managers face lighter but different rules

A portfolio manager must report each investment approach's **time-weighted rate of return** beside one of up to three APMI-prescribed benchmarks for its strategy, show performance relative to peers and have debt priced by APMI-empanelled valuers, rules in force since 1 April 2023† ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sebi-issues-performance-benchmarking-guidelines-for-portfolio-managers-122121600879_1.html); [APMI](https://www.apmiindia.org/storagebox/images/Circulars/APMI-Circular-2-BENCHMARKING.pdf)) and now consolidated in a July 2025 Master Circular that also covers monthly reporting to SEBI ([Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-updates-portfolio-managers-master-circular.html)). The notes did not verify AIF performance benchmarking or Category III concentration and leverage limits, and did not research IRDAI's investment-system rules for insurers at all, so everything this report says about AIFs and insurers is inference. FPIs answer to their home regulators for reporting; in India the rules that bite are the foreign-ownership limits that move MSCI weights.

### Cybersecurity rules set the floor for holding real positions

SEBI's Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF) of August 2024 sorts regulated entities into five categories. Since the April 2025 clarification, portfolio managers with AUM at or below ₹3,000 crore and AIF managers with a combined corpus below ₹3,000 crore self-certify, while larger ones carry mid-size obligations including third-party risk management† ([KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/clarifications-to-cscrf-for-sebi-regulated-entities/); [ELP](https://elplaw.in/leadership/changes-to-sebis-cyber-security-and-cyber-resilience-framework-for-aifs/)), and new critical software must come with a software bill of materials ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/12/sbom-in-indias-regulatory-landscape-building-trust-hrough-transparency.pdf)). The 2023 cloud framework's nine principles include data ownership and localisation ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/mar-2023/framework-for-adoption-of-cloud-services-by-sebi-regulated-entities-res-_68740.html)), but the CSCRF localisation control has been in abeyance since 31 December 2024 and was still described as "non-current" in May 2026† ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-sebi-holds-off-on-data-localisation-cscrf-framework/); [Security Brigade](https://securitybrigade.com/blog/sebi-cscrf-data-localisation-abeyance/)). Whoever hosts the data, the regulated entity stays responsible for its vendors ([CyberNX](https://www.cybernx.com/third-party-risk-assessment-under-sebi-cscrf/)).

## NSE's own terms forbid how the tracker collects its data

### The plain text covers what the tracker does every day

NSE's website terms are clear on their face†:

> "User is prohibited to conduct any systematic or automated data collection activities (including scraping, data mining, data extraction and data harvesting) on or in relation to our Website / Mobile Application."

The same terms bar copying content unless it is offered for download and warn that unauthorised use "may violate copyright, trademark and other applicable laws" ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/static/nse-terms-of-use)), and NSE's analytics portal repeats the ban ([FIXEDIN terms](https://analytics.nseindia.com/assets/images/FIXEDIN-Terms-of-Use.pdf)). Every day the tracker reads corporate announcements, delivery data, the event calendar and corporate actions from NSE's website APIs through a session built to look like a browser. On the plain text, which is as far as the notes go and is not legal advice, that is systematic automated collection "in relation to" the website, and making the requests look like a browser's does not change what they are. **This finding outranks every analytic gap in this report**, because it decides whether anything built on those feeds can be used by an institution at all.

### The exposure is continuity and republication

The first exposure is continuity. Developers report HTTP 403 responses and IP bans when scraping NSE, including from cloud servers ([Unofficed forum](https://forum.unofficed.com/t/nsepython-not-working-in-aws-google-cloud-and-webservers/670)). The tracker has already lost BSE to a bot filter, and a block at NSE would cut four feeds at once, including the calendar and corporate actions added on 10 October. The notes found no court case or enforcement action against a scraper, so the realistic sanction is technical, but an institution cannot write a pipeline that depends on not being noticed into a control document.

The second is republication. The public dashboard, and the data files the daily run commits to the public repository, republish what the pipeline collects ([README](../README.md)). AMFI grants only a "non-exclusive, personal, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, limited and revocable" right for personal, non-commercial use and bars public display and storing "any significant portion" of its site ([AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com/terms-of-use)); NSE requires its written consent before a vendor supplies end-of-day data to its clients "for any type of usage" ([NSE EOD tariff](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/mediaattachment/2026-04/Download_End_of_the_Day_Data_Tariff_20260424120810.pdf)); and the terms of Screener, Yahoo, Google News and the state portals were not checked. If a SEBI-registered portfolio manager ran the tracker in public, SEBI's price-data framework, which since 8 May 2026 imposes a uniform 30-day lag on price data used for education and awareness†, would also need a lawyer's reading ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-revises-time-lag-norms-for-stock-price-data-sharing/)). Buying scraped announcements from a reseller, such as an Apify scraper selling NSE/BSE records at about $50 per 1,000, does not fix provenance ([Apify](https://apify.com/nexgendata/nse-bse-announcements)).

### The licensed route is cheap

The licensed route is cheap by institutional standards and could be cheaper still for a non-commercial user. End-of-day market data plus end-of-day corporate announcements cost about **₹6 lakh a year**†, or **₹11.6 lakh**† with the full corporate data feed in place of the announcements file. NSE's Data Usage and Sharing Policy of October 2024 says its data subsidiary's board "may consider introducing reduced fee arrangements or waivers for Non-Commercial Users", in a section on non-commercial users, research entities and analysts whose text was not captured ([NSE policy](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/NSE_Data_Sharing%26Usage_Policy.pdf)). NSE's separate Non-Display Policy, which governs internal computation such as generating signals, was not read and must be before any contract is signed ([NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/Non_Display_Policy.pdf)). Timing is not the issue: SEBI fined BSE ₹25 lakh in June 2025 for letting paying subscribers see filings before the public website did ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/sebi-imposes-rs-25-lakh-fine-on-bse-for-unequal-data-access-oversight-failures-481863-2025-06-26)), but seconds of lag do not matter to a pipeline that runs once a day.

### The numbers exist, but not by the regulators' methods

The portfolio layer added on 10 October reproduces most of the metric set regulators ask for, but none of it by their method. Its liquidity figures assume a sale of **20% of each stock's average daily value traded over the last month**, with volume measured on NSE alone through Yahoo's ".NS" series and the slowest position setting the pace of a pro-rata sale ([portfolio.py](../analysis/portfolio.py); [liquidity.py](../analysis/liquidity.py); [growth.py](../analysis/growth.py)). Set against the notes' reading of AMFI's method, three of the four differences push the tracker's days-to-sell figure longer.

| Input | Tracker | AMFI (notes' reading) | Effect on the tracker's days-to-sell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participation | 20% of daily value | 10% of three times daily volume | Longer |
| Exchanges | NSE only | NSE and BSE combined | Longer |
| Least-liquid holdings | Included | Least-liquid 20% excluded | Longer |
| Volume window | One month | Three months | Either way |

That makes the tracker's figure a conservative internal warning, but not a number an AMC could publish. Two other disclosed figures are out of reach: the risk-o-meter needs impact cost, which needs order-book depth that no free source gives (the code says so itself), and AMFI's parameter sheet needs investor concentration and turnover, which need unitholder records and a trade history the tracker does not have.

### The other gaps come from missing inputs and missing controls

The remaining quantitative gaps follow from missing inputs rather than missing code. Risk is measured against the Nifty 50 price series from 52 weekly returns; the code will also take a Nifty 500 price series, but without constituent weights there is no Brinson attribution and no benchmark P/E. Returns are a backcast of today's weights, which the code itself warns flatters the book: the first live run read **+27.5% for the year against the Nifty 50's −10.9%**, because the watchlist holds names partly because they rose ([portfolio.py](../analysis/portfolio.py)). There is one book, so no limit summed across a fund house's schemes can be computed. Holdings data comes from the 72-stock watchlist, so a position outside it gets a price but no traded value, market cap or 52-week low, and drops out of the liquidity, ownership and 52-week-low figures ([portfolio.py](../analysis/portfolio.py)). Classifying holdings as large, mid or small cap means ranking every listed company by market capitalisation, with small caps starting at 251st under SEBI's categorisation ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/small-cap-stocks)), which a 72-stock pipeline cannot do.

Two control gaps sit on top. The model book's equal-weight targets are spread over whatever the watchlist holds today, so a name the rotation engine adds becomes a buy order on the next run ([portfolio.py](../analysis/portfolio.py)), a link an institution would need to break with a recorded human decision. Above all, the repository and dashboard are public: the README itself warns that real positions put in the portfolio file become public, there are no accounts or roles, and git history is the only audit trail, so no regulated manager could put client positions into it.

### Where the tracker already goes further than the platforms

The tracker's distinctive work is qualitative, and nothing in the vendor material surveyed documents it. A language model reads PIB releases, nine state governments' policy feeds and Google News for which sectors and companies each measure helps or hurts, and a market-event classifier and an entity graph of partners, suppliers and input costs carry those effects on to second-order names. The platforms' documented language-model features do other jobs: Bloomberg's earnings-call summaries from January 2024 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/bloomberg-launches-ai-powered-earnings-call-summaries)) and its 2025 attribution commentary, FactSet's attribution commentary, Axioma's plain-language stress scenarios and Tijori's Call Monitor. Bloomberg's own analysis of India's tariff exposure was written commentary, not model output ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/insights/risk/building-next-level-risk-capabilities-in-an-age-of-policy-shocks/)), and the one product that could overlap, CRISIL's ForeSight impact-analytics engine, appears in the notes only by name.

Other habits are uncommon too. Each holding carries a written thesis graded Intact, Weakening or Broken from reported numbers, with language-model challenges confirmed by a second, stronger model and scored against hand-labelled sets, although those labels are unreviewed drafts, so the checks' accuracy is not yet established. The rotation engine's picks are measured from the day they were made against the Nifty 50 and sector indices, the fair test the portfolio backcast is not. Every method is stated in readable code, from the 20% participation rate to the refusal to call a beat without consensus, whereas INE2's handbook is client-only and MAC3 India's factors are not public. The tracker also reads filings and announcements for the micro caps in its book (the code names ASM Technologies, SPEL Semiconductor and Faze Three among them), where coverage driven by index membership and broker interest is thinnest, and it runs on GitHub Actions at no cost ([README](../README.md)), against about ₹24–30 lakh a year for a single Bloomberg seat.

One weakness sits inside these strengths. The ownership early warnings rest on Screener's shareholding categories, and aggregators disagree badly on those: for BSE Ltd in June 2026, Anand Rathi shows FII holding at 9.63% where Value Research and Trendlyne show about 21.3% ([Anand Rathi](https://anandrathi.com/stocks/bse-ltd/shareholding-pattern); [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stocks/97436/bse-ltd/shareholding/)). The category mapping should be checked against the exchange XBRL before those warnings are trusted.

Two blockers come first for everyone, and the rest depends on the role the tracker would play. Large AMCs, insurers and FPIs already run an institutional stack (AIM and PORT at the AMCs named above, fund accounting from registrars such as KFintech, global platforms offshore), so for them the tracker is at most a research add-on feeding that stack; a boutique PMS or AIF, for which one Bloomberg seat costs about ₹24–30 lakh a year, could use it as its main analytics platform. The ratings below reflect those roles, and "Blocker" means the institution cannot use the tracker without it. ### Mutual fund AMCs need fund-house aggregation before analytics

After the two blockers, an AMC needs first a holdings feed for every scheme, because the limits that bind it are summed across the fund house (a company's voting capital across all schemes, the sponsor group across all schemes) and a single-book tool cannot compute either. Next comes a universe as wide as its schemes, then the SEBI/AMFI calculators, which matter only if the tracker is to produce a disclosed number: as a research add-on it can leave the risk-o-meter, the AMFI sheet and the Monthly Cumulative Report to existing systems, provided its own figures are labelled internal so they never contradict a published one. Benchmark constituents follow, because the AMFI parameters compare volatility and P/E with the scheme's benchmark. A factor model ranks lower than it looks; no SEBI rule found requires one, though the market expects it, and a 2026 recruiter's brief for an AMC investment-risk role asks for relative VaR, tracking error, factor exposure and attribution across mutual funds, SIFs, PMS, AIFs and offshore funds ([Michael Page](https://www.michaelpage.co.in/job-detail/senior-manager-avp-investment-market-risk-amc/ref/jn-042026-6993308)). Order management and surveillance matter enormously to an AMC but are bought (AIM, Vault), not built into a research tool, and ESG data is a blocker only for ESG schemes.

### PMS providers need time-weighted returns across client books

For a PMS the order after the blockers is time-weighted returns across client books, the APMI benchmark, a wider universe and then pre-trade checks. SEBI's benchmarking rule makes TWRR per investment approach, aggregated across segregated client accounts and set against an APMI-listed benchmark, the core public number, and the tracker has neither transactions nor a TWRR engine. Pre-trade checks matter more here than at an AMC using the tracker as an add-on, because the tracker's order list could become the desk's actual workflow, and extending its existing limit engine to test proposed orders before export is cheap. A factor model is a luxury at this scale, and most boutiques at or below ₹3,000 crore of AUM self-certify under CSCRF, which keeps the compliance burden of running their own platform modest.

### AIFs split by strategy, and insurers need more than equities

A long-only listed-equity Category III AIF looks like a PMS in a fund wrapper, with several schemes, NAV-based returns and a benchmark; a long-short fund needs derivatives, leverage monitoring and factor hedging, which moves assets beyond equities and a factor model up its list, and the concentration and leverage limits that would drive its compliance rules were not verified. An insurer can only ever use the tracker for its equity sleeve, so coverage beyond Indian equities is a blocker for anything more than research, and IRDAI's expectations, unresearched here, would set the rest; the only insurer-related platform evidence in the notes is that Hexagram's fund-accounting clients include insurers.

### FPIs need MSCI benchmarks, and want what only the tracker offers

For an FPI, Indian disclosure formats barely matter and MSCI benchmarks do. It needs MSCI India weights, which foreign-ownership limits and foreign room shape; a global risk model, so India's risk adds up with the rest of an emerging-markets book; returns in dollars; and I/B/E/S-style consensus and globally recognised ESG ratings. What an FPI cannot easily get elsewhere is what the tracker already does, reading state-level policy and promoter, FII and DII shifts on Indian names. One cheap addition would serve FPIs directly: treating MSCI index reviews and foreign-room changes as event signals, given a blog estimate that each percentage point of India's EM weight moves $1.5–2bn of passive money† ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/fpi-flows-may-2026-india-sectoral-analysis)).

### Three budgets, from a fifth of one Bloomberg seat to ₹5 crore

A licensed research tool, or a research add-on at an AMC (steps 0–3), costs **about ₹6–12 lakh a year** in exchange data plus a fundamentals quote, between a fifth and two-fifths of one Bloomberg seat. A boutique PMS or AIF using the tracker as its main platform (steps 0–5 plus one Bloomberg seat for PORT) would spend **roughly ₹33–42 lakh a year** before quotes for fundamentals and benchmark data and, possibly, PORT Enterprise. A mid-size AMC buying the full institutional stack would pay about ₹2.4–2.7 crore for ten Bloomberg seats and an estimated ₹43 lakh–₹2.4 crore for BarraOne, **₹2.8–5.1 crore** in all before AIM, PORT Enterprise, Vault and exchange fees, none of which has a public price; there, the tracker belongs at the research-add-on tier.

### Several high-value steps cost almost nothing once data is licensed

The AMFI stress test can be computed exactly from licensed NSE and BSE volumes and shown beside the tracker's own, deliberately harsher figure. A Taurus-style trigger on a 50% rise in days-to-sell fits the existing new-breach logic, and the passive-breach clock needs only a business-day calendar and a table of Investment Committee decisions. Parsing exchange XBRL shareholding under licence would fix the category mapping the ownership warnings depend on. IIMA's free factor returns can give returns-based style and factor attribution while a vendor model is evaluated, provided the output is labelled as attribution rather than ex-ante risk.

## Conclusion

The binding constraints on the tracker are the least glamorous and the cheapest to fix: provenance and privacy, not factor models. The expensive layers, risk models and order management, are commodities institutions already own or can buy, while the global vendors' liquidity products are calibrated to European, Japanese and US rules and India's binding liquidity measure is a local formula that even PORT and BarraOne users compute separately. The AMFI stress test, the passive-breach clock and APMI-benchmarked TWRR are exactly the India-specific computations a small, transparent, parameterised tool can own, and SEBI's 2026 move from fixed limits to prudential norms that it sets from time to time rewards the same design, but only once the tool runs on data it is allowed to use.

The tracker's defensible asset is not its language model, since every vendor from FactSet to Tijori is adding one, but its corpus and graph: central and state policy tied to named holdings through partners, suppliers and input costs, and theses graded against reported numbers. One decision therefore comes before any purchase, which is what the project is meant to be. If it stays personal, it needs NSE's non-commercial terms and an end to public republication; if it is to serve an institution, it needs licensed data, a private deployment and the regulatory calculators, in that order. Either way, four open questions should be closed before money is committed: what NSE's Non-Display Policy allows, what Screener's terms permit, what IRDAI expects of insurers and what SEBI's AIF limits now are.
